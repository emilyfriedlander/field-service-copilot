# EPA Section 608 Certification Study Guide
*Source: HVAC Technician Training Series — Module 2*

## Overview

EPA Section 608 of the Clean Air Act requires that anyone who purchases, handles, or disposes
of refrigerants must be certified. Technicians must pass a proctored exam for the applicable
equipment type(s).

## Certification Types

| Type       | Equipment Covered                                        |
|------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Type I     | Small appliances (factory-sealed, ≤ 5 lb charge)        |
| Type II    | High-pressure systems (R-22, R-410A, R-134a, etc.)      |
| Type III   | Low-pressure systems (R-11, R-113, R-123, chillers)     |
| Universal  | All of the above — recommended for most field techs     |

---

## Core Legal Requirements

### Venting Prohibition
It is **illegal** to knowingly vent any CFC, HCFC, or HFC refrigerant into the atmosphere
during service, maintenance, repair, or disposal (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F).

Exceptions (very narrow):
- De minimis releases incidental to good-faith efforts to recover
- Mixtures of air and refrigerant where recovery isn't practical and HFC is < 1% by weight

### Required Equipment
- Recovery equipment manufactured after November 15, 1993 must be certified by an
  EPA-approved testing organization (e.g., UL, AHRI)
- Technicians must use certified recovery equipment — personal ownership not required
- Recovery cylinders must be DOT-approved (gray body, yellow top for mixed refrigerants)

### Recovery Requirements Before Opening a System

| System Type      | Compressor Status    | Required Recovery Level         |
|------------------|----------------------|---------------------------------|
| High-pressure, < 200 lb | Working       | 90% (if manufactured before 11/15/93) or 0 psig |
| High-pressure, < 200 lb | Not working   | 80% or 4 in. Hg vacuum         |
| High-pressure, ≥ 200 lb | Any           | 90%                            |
| Low-pressure systems | Any            | 25 mm Hg absolute (29 in. Hg)  |

**Key: Recovery, not recycle, is required in the field.** Recycling (cleaning) requires
certified equipment, typically at a shop.

## Refrigerant Properties & Safety

### Pressure Ratings
- **R-410A** operates at approximately 1.6× the pressures of R-22
- R-410A systems require hoses, gauges, and recovery equipment rated for the higher pressures
- Never use R-22 gauges on R-410A systems

### Refrigerant Cylinders
- Never fill a recovery cylinder beyond **80% of its rated capacity by weight**
- Never apply heat to a cylinder to speed transfer — this is extremely dangerous
- Disposable cylinders must not be refilled
- Cylinders must be stored upright in a cool, ventilated area away from heat sources

### Health & Safety
- Refrigerant vapor displaces oxygen in enclosed spaces — ensure ventilation
- R-410A is an asphyxiant at high concentrations
- A2L refrigerants (R-32, R-454B) are mildly flammable — no open flames, sparks, or smoking
- Contact with liquid refrigerant causes frostbite — wear eye protection and gloves

## Leak Detection & Repair

### EPA Leak Rate Thresholds (Commercial & Industrial)
Systems with ≥ 50 lb charge must be repaired if annual leak rate exceeds:
- **Industrial process refrigeration:** 35%
- **Commercial refrigeration:** 20%
- **Comfort cooling (HVAC):** 15%

Owners must maintain leak inspection records.

### Leak Detection Methods (Best to Acceptable)
1. Electronic leak detector (HFC-rated sensor, most sensitive)
2. UV dye + UV lamp (good for pinpointing after electronic detection)
3. Nitrogen pressure test (systems with no refrigerant)
4. Soap bubbles (basic; use only as secondary confirmation)

**Never use an open flame halide torch on HFC refrigerants** (R-410A, R-32, R-454B).
Reserve for legacy R-22 systems only, and only with proper ventilation.

## Reclaiming vs. Recycling vs. Recovery

| Term       | Definition                                                                     | Where Done       |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Recovery   | Removing refrigerant from a system into an external container                  | Field             |
| Recycling  | Cleaning refrigerant using oil separation + filter-driers for reuse on-site   | Field (cert. equip.) |
| Reclamation| Processing refrigerant to AHRI 700 purity standard (virgin quality)            | Certified facility|

Reclaimed refrigerant is the only type that can be sold or transferred to a different owner.

---

## Record Keeping

Technicians and employers must retain records of:
- Refrigerant type and quantity recovered from each system
- Equipment serial/model number and location
- Date of service
- Name and certification number of technician

Records must be kept for **3 years** and made available to EPA inspectors on request.

## Exam Tips: Frequently Tested Topics

1. The legal definition of "opening an appliance" that triggers recovery requirements
2. Recovery levels by system type and compressor condition (memorize the table above)
3. Cylinder safety: 80% fill limit, no heat, no open flames for HFCs
4. The difference between recovery, recycling, and reclamation
5. Leak rate thresholds for different system categories
6. Proper disposal of small appliances (Type I): self-contained recovery device required
7. R-410A cylinder color: rose/pink (industry standard, not legally required)
